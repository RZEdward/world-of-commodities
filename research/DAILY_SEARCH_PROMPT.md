# Daily and on-demand search instructions

Prompt version: 2026-11-02.6

Use this as the operating prompt for a World of Commodities research run. Read the live workbook and the linked methodology first. The workbook controls names, IDs, owners, rankings and geographic scope; this file controls search behavior. Do not substitute a copied list for the current registry.

## Copy-ready run prompt

> Run a World of Commodities **[daily / weekly-jobs / on-demand / baseline]** search for **[Owner]** as of **[date, Asia/Dubai]**. Use the workbook-generated run brief from `python scripts/prepare_daily_search.py --owner [Owner] --mode [daily|weekly-jobs|on-demand|baseline]` as the current scope snapshot. Search the web and available LinkedIn surfaces as a researcher. Scripts prepare scope, validate and log work; they do not replace unstructured research.
>
> ### A. Follow the current scope and priority
>
> 1. Read the current owner-specific Location Registry and Location Rankings. Use every city with Job Search enabled for job discovery and every city with University Search enabled for programme discovery. Never store a job office or campus outside the relevant registry scope. Do not reactivate excluded cities without owner direction.
> 2. Report newly discovered Hong Kong and Singapore roles first, even when their original posting date is older. Then report other new roles in location-rank order and company-priority order.
> 3. Search every registered job city for roles by title and role family, including employers absent from Company Registry. This broad city sweep is the safety net against registry omissions. Use the registered company list as an additional targeted search layer, not as the boundary of job discovery.
> 4. Search all roles that could reasonably fit Ross’s profile, including power/gas analysis, forecasting, modelling, trading, quantitative research, quant trading, portfolio/asset optimization, market fundamentals, energy research, and adjacent commodities or quantitative roles. Do not restrict the run to the current company registry or to exact title matches. Do not omit low-fit roles solely because of fit; score and label them.
> 5. In China, Japan and South Korea, search in English and the relevant native language: Simplified Chinese for mainland China, Japanese for Japan, and Korean for South Korea. Translate queries and source material as needed. Ross is learning Mandarin and does not read Japanese or Korean, so explain material non-English findings in English and preserve the original source link and translated search terms. Do not assume language fluency for any job requirement; record the employer’s stated requirement.

> ### A1. Mandatory completion gate for a requested full/baseline sweep
>
> A **weekly-jobs sweep** means all registered Job Search cities receive broad title-led searches, all active ranked employers receive an individual employer/ATS check, every mapped in-scope employer-city pair is considered, and all existing active job rows are refreshed. Keep two independent employer counts: (a) employers represented in `company_location_pairs` and mapped city pairs, and (b) the full active ranked Company Registry. Missing office mappings never excuse skipping the employer: inspect its careers/ATS once for every registered city it serves, even if the Office Registry currently has no row. Use broad employer careers inventories and city/title queries as complementary layers. In the 2026-11-02 completed sweep, 288 employers accounted for 977 mapped pairs and 142 additional active ranked employers had no mapped target-city office; therefore the required totals were 430/430 employer checks, 977/977 mapped-pair checks, and 14/14 registered-city searches. These are run-specific counts; always regenerate them from the current workbook scope rather than reuse them. Record a separate explicit outcome for each employer and retain source-access limitations. Do not claim mapped office pairs exhaust actual employer presence. This weekly job sweep excludes university discovery, office-address research, review research, and recruiter-connection mapping; those have separate weekly/monthly/quarterly batches.
>
> A request for a **full search**, **baseline**, or **initial complete opportunities set** means finish every due batch enumerated in the generated run brief, not merely run a daily high-yield subset. Before research, record the actual start time. Treat the due-batch list as a checklist and mark each batch `Complete`, `Attempted — source unavailable`, or `Not started`; never mark a batch complete based on a broad search that did not cover its listed scope. A baseline includes city-by-city role discovery in all registered job cities; the full ranked-employer career/ATS check across active tiers and mapped in-scope offices; refresh of every open role and near-term deadline; all registered university-search cities and urgent programmes; and every due registry, recruiter, official market-data, speculative-application, and review batch listed in the brief. Office address verification is a separate weekly maintenance batch; university programme discovery is monthly/admissions-cycle. Search registered employers in rank order, and investigate active power/gas participants evidenced by market sources; selective company inclusion still applies.
>
> Do not close a requested full/baseline/weekly-jobs run while any required job city, ranked employer, in-scope mapped company-city pair, or open role is `Not started`. Continue in manageable regional batches and resume from the checklist if work spans turns. If a genuine tool, access, or runtime limit prevents continuation, save the exact checkpoint and mark the run `Incomplete — resume required`; do not present that as a completed weekly sweep or initial dataset. An inaccessible page is an attempted batch only when the source and failure are recorded; substitute another credible source where possible. The final closeout must state completed/attempted/not-started counts and elapsed wall-clock time. A daily run is complete only for that day's due batches and must not be described as a full baseline.
> The completion ledger must independently reconcile: `active ranked employers checked / total`; `mapped company-city pairs checked / total`; `registered Job Search cities searched / total`; and `existing active job rows refreshed / total`. Every ranked employer needs a named outcome (live role, no relevant role in checked sources, identity mismatch, no target-city presence, or source unavailable after alternatives); an aggregate company count without a row-level ledger is insufficient. Count only a real vacancy once per distinct requisition and registered city, while creating separate role-city rows for each valid city. Keep credible-but-unconfirmed leads in the research log with a verification state; do not silently promote or discard them.

Search city + role family + hiring/careers terms and native-language equivalents for China, Japan and South Korea. Capture actual queries and results. Group offices under one employer-source check when appropriate; log coverage by both company and covered cities. Prioritize Tier 1A/1B first but complete every active tier before calling the weekly sweep complete. For a company with no credible relevant desk, record a concise no-relevant-desk result instead of deep role research; do not silently skip it.
> 3. A lightweight **daily** run, if requested, checks newly surfaced roles and changes via high-yield alerts/searches, with Hong Kong and Singapore first; it does not repeat all ranked-employer checks. Do not label a daily run the weekly-jobs sweep.

The URL scanner can flag reachability but cannot tell whether the role remains open. Open and interpret the employer/recruiter page before changing status.

Use the applicable regulator, exchange, system operator, balancing mechanism, transmission/storage/LNG capacity auction, and FiT/CfD/renewables/ancillary-service sources. Record what the source proves and does not prove. Recheck a source when it publishes new data or the log says it is due.

Include a route only if an official employer page explicitly accepts general, speculative or talent-pool applications. Use official university/programme/admissions/scholarship pages for course facts, eligibility, tuition, living costs, intake and deadlines. Distinguish international-applicant pathways from domestic entrance-exam cohorts; confirm language of instruction, nationality/degree eligibility, required examinations, application portal, and whether that cycle is still open before calling a programme actionable. Review recruiter-company and recruiter-person discovery, LinkedIn connections/following, office-by-office mapping, and Glassdoor/Reddit research on a rotating cadence rather than redoing each daily. > ### C. Keep company scope selective
>

>

## Search cadence and learning rule

These are starting cadences; Search Coverage Log records evidence for adjusting them:

- **Daily when requested:** lightweight new-role discovery and changes; Hong Kong/Singapore first. Does not repeat the full company list.
- **Weekly:** full ranked-employer career/ATS sweep across registered offices and all 14 job cities; open-role/status/deadline refresh; office-address research batch; unsolicited application routes; recruiter/company discovery and accessible LinkedIn review; power/gas company discovery.
- **Monthly or admissions-cycle:** university programme discovery/refresh across all University Search cities and official market-participation datasets whose records update periodically.
- **Quarterly or when new evidence appears:** Glassdoor/Reddit company reputation review. Do not spend daily effort searching for unchanged reviews.
- **Event-driven:** new auction/market notices, regulator releases, official company expansion or desk news, and a posting or status signal.

Do not downgrade a useful search family after one quiet run. Consider reducing frequency only after at least three comparable, logged runs show low yield, and retain a periodic check or event trigger. Increase frequency after meaningful yield, a change in source publication schedule, or a user request. Keep a separate on-demand full pass available.

## Search Coverage Log fields

Each row represents a repeatable source-family and market/query batch. Use the same family and scope labels between runs so yield is comparable. Efficient query packs may combine cities or source platforms, but record actual coverage; do not mark an unsearched city or family as checked.

`Run ID`; `Checked On`; `Search Family`; `Source / Platform`; `Market / Location Scope`; `Company Category / Rank Band`; `Query Pack / Search Terms`; `Language`; `Cadence Used`; `Items Reviewed`; `Relevant Leads`; `New Roles`; `Updated Records`; `Registry Candidates`; `Duplicates`; `Rejected`; `Elapsed Minutes`; `Yield / Evidence Notes`; `Cadence Recommendation`; `Next Review On`; `Source URLs`.

Run-level elapsed time and usage visibility live in Search Runs. Search Coverage Log records enough detail to decide whether a source family should be daily, weekly, monthly, event-driven, or paused. Keep the search query/result batch aggregated; do not turn the log into a copy of every result.

## Learning loop and prompt maintenance

At the close of every run, review Search Runs and Search Coverage Log against actual source yield, time cost, duplicate rate, and the reason useful leads were found or missed. Feed repeatable lessons into the next run: improve query wording and native-language synonyms, reorder sources, add a reliable source family, clarify evidence checks, or adjust cadence. Update this prompt and any affected playbook/script when the change is reusable; put the date, version, reason, and affected section in the change log below. Keep the owner-approved scope, scoring weights, inclusion threshold, and application-status rules intact unless Ross changes them. A single quiet run is not enough to reduce coverage: use the existing three-comparable-runs rule. Leave unsearched batches explicitly marked as gaps; never imply complete coverage. At each run's closeout, refresh the dashboard feed and static build, and record whether that succeeded.
